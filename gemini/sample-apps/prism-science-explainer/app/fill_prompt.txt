MOTION CONTRACT — THE #1 REQUIREMENT, READ TWICE:
The shell AUTOPLAYS the moment the instrument appears. The single most common failure is an instrument that looks beautiful and is completely dead: the learner sees a still picture, presses Play, and nothing happens. That is a total failure of the output regardless of how good it looks. Avoid it as follows.

- `step()` IS MANDATORY and must contain real logic. An empty `step(state, dt){}`, or one that only holds a comment, is a broken submission. It must mutate something you stashed on `state` in `init()`.
- THE ACCEPTANCE TEST — apply it to your own code before you answer: *"With every slider held perfectly still, is a LARGE, OBVIOUS part of the picture still visibly different at t=5s, at t=30s, and at t=60s?"* If the answer is no, your instrument is dead and you must redesign it. The motion must never stop or settle.
- MOTION MUST BE STRUCTURAL, NOT DECORATIVE. The thing that moves has to be the thing that teaches. A static diagram with a few small drifting dots sprinkled on top is the classic failure and is NOT acceptable: sparkles, dust motes, or 40 tiny 2px particles bolted onto an otherwise fixed scene do not count as animation. The primary visual — the wave, the beam, the orbit, the population, the wavefront, the sweeping value, the filling bar, the marker travelling along the curve — must itself be a function of time and must sweep across a substantial fraction of the canvas.
- NEVER SETTLE. Many natural processes converge (a ball rolling to the bottom of a valley, a system reaching equilibrium). Those go still after a second or two and then Play genuinely does nothing. If your process converges, you MUST make it loop forever. Use one of these:
    * cycle it: drive the scene from a periodic quantity, e.g. `const phase = state.t * speed;` and let the visual oscillate/orbit/wave with `Math.sin(phase)`;
    * re-seed it: when the process has converged or a particle exits the frame, respawn it — `if (state.progress > 0.999) this.init(state);` or recycle individual particles;
    * sweep it: continuously march a value (a scanning line, a growing curve, an advancing front) across the frame and wrap it around at the end.
- Something must be moving IMMEDIATELY, in the first second, without the user touching anything. Do not require a click to start. Do not spend the interesting motion in the first 200ms and then hold a static end-state: if your process has a transient, stretch it to run over 4–10 seconds, then loop it.
- BIG MOTION, NOT MICROSCOPIC MOTION. The moving element must sweep a LARGE fraction of the frame — think a third of the width or height, not a few pixels. A pendulum oscillating by 1°, a marker creeping along by 3px, a barely-perceptible shimmer: all count as dead. Scale your geometry (and your amplitudes) so the movement is obvious across the room during a live demo.
- CHOOSE DEFAULTS THAT ALREADY SHOW THE PHENOMENON. The `default` of every parameter must put the system in its INTERESTING state on arrival — not at rest, not at zero amplitude, not off-resonance, not at a degenerate edge of the range. If the concept is a build-up (resonance, growth, cascade), start it PART-WAY IN so the learner immediately sees the characteristic behaviour, and let the sliders explore away from it. Never ship a default where the honest caption would be "nothing is happening yet".
